# natural-chinese-writing

## Purpose

Use this skill when the task requires Chinese that feels natural, alive, and usable.

This skill is not for making writing sound more "polished" in a generic way.
It is for making Chinese sound less generated and more real.

The target is:
- natural
- direct
- clear
- information-dense
- human-sounding
- readable without feeling templated

## Trigger rule (MANDATORY)

**凡涉及中文文章/文档的写作或编辑，必定触发此 skill。** 包括但不限于：复盘、反思、论文、报告、教程、公告、演讲稿、公众号文章、个人陈述、申请材料、叙事写作、分析写作。

**也包括以下「看起来不是写作」的任务**：合并多份文档、清理/梳理/整理/重构文档、多稿合一、把材料整理成存档、翻译成中文、把零散笔记整理成文档。表面是文件整理、实际产出中文段落的任务，同样触发。

判断标准很简单：**产出的中文超过一个段落** → 加载此 skill + references/isaac-writing-rules.md。

不需要判断「写得好不好」「有没有 AI 味」——所有中文写作一律先走这条流水线，宁可多走一步，不可漏掉。

## Do not use this skill when

仅以下情况豁免：
- 回复中引用/展示他人原文（保留原样）
- 纯代码、命令行输出、技术日志
- 古典文言文创作
- 用户明确要求保留特定正式/官方文风

## Core principle

Natural Chinese is not just shorter Chinese.

Natural Chinese keeps the real meaning while removing:
- generated rhythm
- fake smoothness
- customer-service tone
- over-explicit rhetorical scaffolding
- translated-English sentence logic
- empty transitions that say nothing new

The goal is not to simplify thought.
The goal is to remove artificial language.

## Working style

Favor:
- direct statements
- natural sentence rhythm
- real emphasis
- concrete wording
- spoken-feeling modern Chinese when appropriate
- strong verbs
- compression without flattening meaning

Avoid:
- over-packaging
- fake symmetry
- padded recaps
- abstract noun piles
- empty conclusion markers
- AI-style emotional calibration language
- stiff "service" politeness

## What natural Chinese means here

Natural Chinese in this skill usually has these properties:

- it starts from the matter itself
- it does not keep re-announcing what it is about to do
- it does not sound like it is managing the reader's emotions
- it does not explain every transition
- it preserves the writer's actual point instead of washing it into neutral gray prose
- it sounds like a smart person talking clearly, not a brand voice

## Rewrite goals

When rewriting, optimize for four things at the same time:

### 1. Meaning retention
Do not lose the actual claim, distinction, or structure.

### 2. Natural rhythm
Make the sentence movement feel like real Chinese, not translated English.

### 3. Information density
Remove fluff without removing meaning.

### 4. Voice integrity
Do not sterilize the text into generic corporate prose.

## Common problems to detect

Before rewriting, identify which problems are present.

### A. AI-style packaging

Examples of the pattern:
- first spending too long framing the task
- telling the reader what is about to happen instead of doing it
- adding emotional cushioning with no informational value
- using overly balanced summary lines that do not change understanding

### B. Translated-English rhythm

Examples of the pattern:
- too many explicit logical connectors
- subject-heavy sentence structure that feels English-first
- abstract phrases that sound okay in English but dead in Chinese
- sentence endings that sound like literal translation

### C. Consultant / corporate language

Examples of the pattern:
- strategy-sounding abstractions
- inflated nouns instead of real verbs
- "抓手 / 赋能 / 闭环" type language
- polished emptiness

### D. Customer-service tone

Examples of the pattern:
- excessive politeness padding
- fake warmth
- overuse of "可以 / 帮你 / 让我们来..."
- tone that sounds like support staff rather than a collaborator

### E. Meta-reasoning narration

Examples of the pattern:
- narrating that the conclusion is being updated
- repeatedly signposting reasoning steps in a self-conscious way
- saying "so the conclusion now becomes..." instead of just stating the conclusion

### F. Overwritten written-Chinese stiffness

Examples of the pattern:
- too many bookish turns
- heavy punctuation and quotation-driven rhythm
- too much distance from actual spoken thought
- correct but lifeless expression

### G. Over-polished rewriting

Examples of the pattern:
- improving sentences that were not actually blocking the reader
- replacing the author's plain observation with a polished slogan
- deleting small oral traces such as hesitation, self-correction, sentence-final particles, or uneven rhythm
- adding a sharper image, time, place, number, or scene that the author did not provide
- making the text smoother while making it less like the person who wrote it

## Workflow

Follow this order.

### 1. Identify the target mode

Figure out what kind of Chinese the task needs.

Common target modes:
- plain spoken explanatory Chinese
- concise analytical Chinese
- natural reflective / narrative Chinese
- clean report-style Chinese without AI tone
- classroom-friendly plain Chinese
- direct collaborator-style Chinese

Do not use the same rewrite style for every task.

### 2. Preserve the spine

Before editing, identify:
- the main claim
- the key distinction
- the emotional force if any
- the logical spine
- what must not be lost

Do not start cutting until you know what the text is actually trying to say.

### 3. Remove artificial scaffolding

Cut things like:
- obvious setup lines
- repeated framing
- empty recap sentences
- low-information transitions
- fake neutrality
- service-style cushioning
- summary lines that only restate what was already said

### 4. Rebuild sentence rhythm

Adjust for Chinese rhythm:
- move key information forward
- prefer natural order over English order
- reduce explicit connectors when the meaning is already clear
- break sentences where needed
- combine sentences where the thought belongs together

### 5. Replace dead abstractions with real language

Where possible:
- use verbs instead of abstract nouns
- use concrete distinctions instead of broad labels
- use actual examples or mechanisms instead of vague praise

### 6. Check voice

After rewriting, ask:
- does this still sound like a person, not a machine?
- does it still sound like someone with judgment?
- did the text lose its edge or energy?
- did it become too neutral?

If yes, restore force without restoring clutter.

## Special handling patterns

### Pattern A: Analytical writing

For analysis:
- say the conclusion early
- support it with concrete distinctions
- remove grand framing
- keep the logic visible but not over-explained

Do not write like a consulting slide deck turned into prose.

### Pattern B: Narrative / reflection writing

For narrative or reflective text:
- keep the movement of thought
- keep tension and shift
- keep emotional truth if it exists
- remove only the dead language, not the human texture

Do not clean the life out of the writing.

### Pattern C: Explanation writing

For explanation:
- prefer simple Chinese first
- define terms only as much as needed
- use real examples early
- make it sayable out loud

Do not turn explanation into lecture prose.

### Pattern D: Critical / judgment writing

For critique:
- be direct
- avoid fake balance
- state what is wrong clearly
- explain why in concrete terms

Do not soften the point into harmlessness.

### Pattern E: Isaac-specific rules

When writing for or reviewing Isaac's Chinese prose, additional hard constraints apply beyond the general principles above. **Load `references/isaac-writing-rules.md`** — it covers corrective-structure bans, anti-labeling rules, breathing rhythm, quote format, and the full multi-pass review pipeline (natural-chinese-writing → humanizer → Isaac rules → breathing → quote check).

### Pattern F: English → Chinese translation with voice preservation

When the source text is English and must be rendered as natural Chinese in the user's voice, extra vigilance is needed because English idiom smuggles in banned patterns:

**Pre-translation scan** — identify these before writing the first Chinese sentence:
- English "not X, but Y" / "not from X, from Y" → this is the most dangerous pattern. English uses it everywhere for clean contrast, but direct translation produces 「不是X，而是Y」which is banned. Restructure: drop the negative half and state the positive directly, or use 「它本身就……」「跟……无关」「反倒是」.
- English abstract nouns (impulse, tendency, synthesis, running into, the act of) → unpack into concrete verbs before translating. "The impulse to add things" → 「想加东西的冲动」(keep the concrete 想加, don't nominalize to 「添加的冲动」).
- English passive → Chinese active. "The mechanism was unlocked by..." → 「这个洞察直接解锁了……」not 「机制被……解锁了」.
- English stacked possessives and "of"-chains → break into short clauses. "The difference between a tap that feels intentional and one that feels awkward" → 「一个碰了觉得有仪式感的动作，和一个碰了觉得尴尬的动作，差别很微妙」.

**Post-translation verification** — after drafting:
1. Scan for any 「不是」that isn't a factual narrative or first-person self-reflective statement (only the explicit exceptions in isaac-writing-rules.md rule 5).
2. Read aloud: if a sentence feels like it was thought in English first and then rendered in Chinese, it probably was — restructure.
3. Run the full Isaac rules check (矫正句式 grep, breathing check, quote check) as if the text were originally written in Chinese.

**Pitfall**: English source text that already sounds natural and personal in English can trick you into thinking the direct translation will sound natural in Chinese. It won't. Translation adds a layer of rhythmic foreignness that the reader will feel even if every individual word is correct. Always treat translated text as needing the same de-translation pass you'd give AI-generated Chinese.

### Pattern G: Editing another person's draft

When the user brings their own text and asks for polishing, rewriting, trimming, or "make it less AI-like", preserve the person behind the draft. The best edit is often almost invisible.

Use these rules:
- Change less than you want to. Every edit must answer: what was actually tripping the reader here?
- Treat rough edges as possible voice before treating them as defects. Oral traces, repeated words, uneven sentence length, and small particles may carry hesitation, self-mockery, breath, or stance.
- Do not add quotable lines. A neat antithesis, polished metaphor, or elegant summary is a warning sign if it did not come from the author.
- Do not replace plain observation with copywriting. A flat sentence can be the author's calm judgment.
- Do not invent concreteness. Avoid adding scenes, times, numbers, examples, or images that are not already implied by the text.
- When unsure, use plain description. Say what happened, keep adjectives light, and avoid upgrading the prose into performance.
- After editing, list what changed and why. Mark uncertain edits as reversible.

Check only the sentences you changed, not the whole original. Look for:
- inflated meaning: 「体现了」「彰显了」「折射出」「重新定义」「时代浪潮」
- promotional tone: 「赋能」「打造」「极致体验」「不容错过」「深厚底蕴」
- formulaic structure: 「不是X，而是Y」, forced three-part parallelism, fake 「从X到Y」 ranges, slogan-like 「X 是 Y 的语言」
- format residue: mechanical bolding, decorative emoji, a filler sentence under a heading before the actual point
- service tone: 「希望这对你有帮助」「好问题」「让我们深入探讨」「未来可期」
- padding: 「值得注意的是」「需要指出的是」「在某种程度上」「不得不说」

If a changed sentence now looks more fluent but less owned by the author, restore the author's version or make a smaller edit.

### Pattern H: 发现者叙事 — AI 高频指纹

「我发现了一个没人发现的东西」「没有一个人意识到」「我是第一个……」——这个叙事框架本身是 AI 生成中文的高频模式。

人当然也会用发现者叙事。区别在于人说完之后会跟具体证据、方法论说明、自嘲或不确定性。AI 说完就没了，只留下一个孤零零的宣告。

**检测三步**：遇到发现者叙事时，检查后面有没有：
- 具体的方法论说明（怎么选的论文、什么纳入/排除标准）
- 反证考虑（为什么可能不是这样）
- 自嘲或不确定性（「可能」「至少」「我还在验证」）

三项全无 → AI 指纹，改写。

**改写方向**：
- 「我发现了一个没人命名的新领域」 → 「我注意到多个学科正在同时逼近同一个问题」
- 「没有一个人意识到」 → 「目前尚未形成统一命名」
- 「我是第一个……」 → 删掉，不必说

原则：**让人通过你的证据自己得出「这是个新领域」的判断，不要替读者下结论。** 你负责把材料摆清楚，结论是读者的事。

## Output modes

When useful, you may internally think in these modes:

- keep meaning, improve naturalness
- compress without losing substance
- make more spoken
- make less AI-like
- make less formal
- make more forceful
- make cleaner but keep the original energy

Choose the mode that fits the task.
Do not automatically apply all modes at once.

## Style rules

Prefer:
- short to medium-length sentences
- natural modern Chinese
- clean transitions
- direct claims
- examples when helpful
- rhythm that can be spoken aloud

Avoid:
- robotic symmetry
- repetitive "首先 / 其次 / 最后" unless truly useful
- overuse of "对于…来说"
- overuse of "本质上 / 实际上 / 从某种意义上说" when they add nothing
- formulaic recap lines
- fake emotional warmth
- decorative elegance without information value

## What to preserve

Do not accidentally remove:
- real distinctions
- strong judgments
- useful nuance
- the writer's actual personality
- the sharpness of a critique
- the momentum of a thought process

The text should become more alive, not more generic.

## Quality checklist

Before finishing, check:

- Does this sound like something a real person would actually say or write?
- Did I remove AI-style packaging?
- Did I keep the actual meaning and force?
- Did I reduce translated-English rhythm?
- Did I avoid washing everything into gray formal prose?
- If I edited the user's own text, did I preserve their stance, breath, and odd but meaningful choices?
- Did I avoid adding polished claims, fake specificity, or rhetorical performance?
- Is the result clearer, not just shorter?
- Would the user feel this sounds more like them or more usable for them?

If not, revise the rhythm and tone, not just the wording.

## Failure modes

Watch for these common failures:

### 1. Over-cleaning
Making the text technically clean but emotionally dead.

### 2. Under-cleaning
Leaving obvious AI-style framing and empty transitions.

### 3. Meaning loss
Cutting too aggressively and losing real distinctions.

### 4. Formality drift
Trying to make the text "better" by making it more written and less human.

### 5. Tone mismatch
Using the same naturalness setting for analysis, narrative, critique, and explanation.

### 6. Neutralization
Removing edge, force, or personality in the name of smoothness.

### 7. Spoken fake
Trying to sound spoken by adding low-value filler instead of fixing rhythm.

### 8. Over-authoring
Making the text more impressive by replacing the author's voice with yours.

## Final instruction

Your job is not to make the Chinese prettier.

Your job is to make it feel like a real person with a real mind wrote it, while keeping the substance fully intact.
