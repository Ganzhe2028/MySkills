# Coding Guide

Use this reference when analyzing meaningful-life interviews, especially interviews about AI, learning, ability, self-worth, work, purpose, and identity.

## Core Codes

| Code | Simple meaning | Evidence signals | Do not confuse with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Why someone keeps doing something and what future it points toward | "I learn this because...", "In the future I want...", "This lets me..." | Not simple interest. Interest is liking; purpose is direction. |
| Value / Values | What someone believes is important, right, or worth choosing | "Creativity is more important", "Real experience matters", "People should stay curious", "We cannot fully depend on AI" | Not only moral values. It can be priorities in life choices. |
| Efficacy | The person believes they can do or affect something | "I can judge whether AI is wrong", "I can debug", "I can use AI to make my idea real", "My project got feedback" | Not self-worth. This is ability, not value as a person. |
| Self-Worth | How the person confirms that they have value | "Even if my grades are bad...", "I know what I like even without recognition", "I can affect others" | Not general confidence. It asks whether the self feels worthwhile. |
| Crisis / Exploration | The person starts doubting, comparing, rethinking, or exploring | "If AI can do this, why should I learn?", "I used to think X, now I am not sure", "I am exploring another path" | Crisis does not require collapse. It can be identity-level reconsideration. |
| Commitment | The person forms a choice and keeps investing in it | "I will still learn writing", "I will keep making art", "AI is a partner, but I must keep judgment" | Not casual liking. Look for stable reason or action. |
| Identity Moratorium | The person is exploring without a stable answer yet | "I do not know what I will do", "It depends on my future job", "I am still thinking about my relationship with AI" | Not failure. It is active searching. |
| Identity Achievement | The person has explored and formed a relatively stable answer | "I used to think skills mattered most, now I think judgment matters more, so I will..." | Not perfect maturity. It just means the answer is stable in this area. |
| Foreclosure | The person has a chosen answer without much personal exploration | "Because parents / school / everyone says so, I will..." | Strong external answer, little personal exploration. |
| Diffusion | The person shows neither exploration nor commitment | "I do not know", "I have not thought about it", "Whatever" | Do not label weak expression as diffusion if there is real thought underneath. |

## Common Confusions

### Purpose vs Value

Purpose asks: where am I going?

Value asks: why is this worth doing?

Example:

- "I learn English so I can reach more cultures in the future." -> Purpose
- "Language learning is worth it because it helps me understand another culture." -> Value

### Efficacy vs Self-Worth

Efficacy asks: can I do this?

Self-Worth asks: am I worthwhile?

Example:

- "I can tell when AI is making things up." -> Efficacy
- "Even if my grades are not good, I know I still have strengths." -> Self-Worth

### Crisis vs Commitment

Crisis / Exploration is doubt, comparison, or rethinking.

Commitment is choice, investment, and continuing.

Example:

- "If AI can write essays, why should I still learn writing?" -> Crisis / Exploration
- "AI writes fast, but I will still learn writing because human writing is more alive." -> Commitment

### Moratorium vs Achievement

Both include exploration.

Moratorium means the person has not settled yet.

Achievement means the person has explored and now has a relatively stable answer.

Example:

- "I still do not know what my relationship with AI will be; it depends on the job." -> Moratorium
- "AI can execute, but I need to keep judgment, need analysis, and real relationship." -> Achievement leaning

## Fast Coding Questions

Ask these questions before assigning a label:

1. Is this quote about why the person learns or continues? Consider Purpose.
2. Is it about what is important, worth doing, or right? Consider Value.
3. Is it about whether the person can do, judge, create, or affect something? Consider Efficacy.
4. Is it about how the person knows they have value? Consider Self-Worth.
5. Does it show doubt, comparison, change, or rethinking? Consider Crisis / Exploration.
6. Does it show a stable choice or continuing action after doubt? Consider Commitment.

## Pattern Rules

A pattern should usually meet all of these:

- appears in at least two interviewees
- states exactly how many interviewees support it, such as "3 of 6 interviewees"
- can be written as a full sentence
- has concrete evidence
- explains something meaningful, not only a topic
- can help the final presentation answer "what did we discover?"

Do not write vague pattern quantities such as "several interviewees," "many interviewees," "some interviewees," "most interviewees," "all interviewees," "both interviewees," "很多受访者," "一些受访者," "多数受访者," "大部分受访者," or "受访者都..." by themselves. These phrases are too imprecise for evidence-based analysis. Always count the supporting interviewees and include the count in the sentence.

This rule applies to the whole final output, not only the pattern column. Compare/contrast rows, evidence summaries, and conclusions also need exact counts when they describe prevalence. Write "2 of 2 members in Group 1" instead of "both members," and write "6 of 6 interviewees" instead of "all interviewees."

Counts must be based on distinct interviewees who substantively support the exact claim, not on how many examples are convenient to cite. Before using a count, mark each interviewee as:

- Present: direct support; count this person
- Weak / partial: related but not enough for the full claim; do not count unless the claim is revised
- Absent: no usable support; do not count

If a pattern says "6 of 6 interviewees," all 6 must have at least one usable interviewee statement supporting that exact pattern. If a pattern says "4 of 6 interviewees," the other 2 should be genuinely weak or absent for that exact claim, not merely omitted from the evidence cell.

## Academic Support Audit

For research-style or teacher-assessed work, do not move from evidence extraction to final pattern writing until every candidate pattern has this audit:

| Interviewee | Status | Evidence decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | Present | Directly supports the exact claim; count this person |
| Name | Weak / partial | Related to the topic but missing part of the claim; do not count unless the claim is revised |
| Name | Absent | No usable interviewee statement; do not count |

The final denominator is the number of distinct interviewees, not the number of transcript files and not the number of quotes. The numerator is the number marked Present.

Common count mistakes:

- Counting the number of examples shown in the evidence cell instead of all distinct interviewees who support the claim.
- Assuming omitted interviewees are absent without checking their transcripts.
- Broadening a pattern after seeing weak evidence, then forgetting to update the exact claim.
- Using a theory label such as Self-Worth to count evidence that only shows Efficacy, agency, or AI-risk awareness.
- Writing "all," "both," or "many" in compare/contrast rows even when the pattern column uses exact counts.

Before final delivery, ask:

1. What exact claim is being counted?
2. Which interviewees are Present, Weak / partial, and Absent?
3. Does every Present case have direct interviewee wording?
4. Are Weak / partial cases excluded or has the claim been honestly revised?
5. Does the final table distinguish support count from selected evidence examples?

Weak:

- "AI as tool"
- "Several interviewees see AI as an efficient tool."

Better:

- "4 of 6 interviewees see AI as an efficient tool for execution, but they still think humans need to keep judgment and responsibility."

Weak:

- "Self-worth"
- "Some interviewees rely less on external recognition."

Better:

- "3 of 6 interviewees rely less on grades or external recognition when they can see their work affecting real people."

## Evidence Rules

Good evidence:

- comes from the interviewee
- keeps enough wording to show meaning
- is tied to a named person
- directly supports the claim
- can justify why that interviewee is counted as Present

Weak evidence:

- only repeats the interviewer question
- is too vague
- is only a yes/no answer without explanation
- is a polished theory summary rather than lived experience
- is related to the topic but does not support the exact pattern sentence

If there is not enough evidence, weaken the claim or remove the pattern.

If the total number of interviewees is unclear, first count the distinct interviewees from the source list. If one person has multiple transcript files, count that person once.
